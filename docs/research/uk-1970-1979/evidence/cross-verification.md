## A. High-confidence findings (cross-verified across independent workers/sources)
- A1. UK ticket tiers 1974 (50p cheap / ~£2 average big band; promoter
  ~1/3 + 8% VAT before band's share) — Sounds, 9 Nov 1974 (reprint);
  corroborated by VAT rate table (8% in 1974-79, parliamentary library).
  Dims 1, 2, 8.
- A2. Fee-vs-cost inversion: 10cc ~£1,000/gig with ~£750 costs by
  showtime (1974, Sounds). Dims 1, 2 (same primary source; documented
  contemporary reporting).
- A3. Two-tier studio market c.1976-78: London 16-track ~£27-30/hour
  (Studio Sound Oct 1976 trade ad + Spaceward co-founder interview) vs
  Cambridge package ~£100/16-hour day (~£6-7/hour); Scritti Politti's
  sleeve accounts (14 hrs = £98) corroborate the provincial package.
  Dims 3, 5.
- A4. DIY single economics 1977-78: Desperate Bicycles £153 all-in for
  500 copies (3 hrs studio, pressing, sleeves), £210 profit on first
  500; Scritti Politti itemized sleeve list (recording £98 + mastering
  £40 + pressing £369.36 for 2,500 @13p + plating £27). Dims 3, 6
  (Wikipedia citing NME; academic reproduction of sleeve). Independent
  cases, consistent magnitude (hundreds of £).
- A5. Wage baselines: NES full-time manual men £26.8/wk (1970) → £55.7
  (1975); non-manual £35.8 → £68.4; BoE per-person series £17.51 (1970
  Q1) → £74.41 (1979 Q4). Dim 2, official statistics.
- A6. Dole vs wage: benefit for family claimant £27.50-£34.30/wk (1976,
  Hansard) vs manual average £66.97 (Oct 1976, ILO study). Dim 7, two

- A7. New-act major deal anchor: The Jam £6,000 + 6% royalty, Polydor
  Feb 1977; option exercised to 13% (4 albums/4 years). Dim 4
  (Independent obituary reprint). Key beginner-tier datapoint.
- A8. Punk signing spree: Sex Pistols EMI Oct 1976 ~£40,000 (dims 4, 6 —

- A9. DIY manufacture at unit level: pressing 13p/copy at 2,500-run

- A10. Studio-session brevity: Buzzcocks' Spiral Scratch cut in 3 hrs

## B. Contradictions & resolutions
- B1. "Average wage 1970" spread (£17.51 / £26.8 / £35.8 / ~£32):
  definitional (per-person vs manual men vs non-manual vs retrospective)
  — RESOLVED as definitional; recommend NES full-time men as game
  baseline, label it.
- B2. Sniffin' Glue first issue: 50 copies print run vs "sold about 300"
  (Christie's lot) — UNRESOLVED (print vs sales possible but unproven).
- B3. BPI thresholds: value-based (£1m platinum) vs unit-based (200k
  gold/100k silver) at 1973; alleged switch to units (300k/100k/60k) Jan
  1978 vs value quoted until 1981 — CONTESTED; resolve pass pending.
- B4. UK album price trajectory: £2.50 (1974) → £2.99 (1977) vs £2.50-3
  (1970) → £4.99 (1979) recollections — CONTESTED; resolve pass pending.
- B5. Spiral Scratch total cost: ~£750 vs ~£500 accounts — UNRESOLVED
  (different accountings).
- B6. Pistols A&M payout: "at least £75,000" vs "nearer £125,000 incl
  severance" — reported range; treat as range.
- B7. Bickershaw failure cause: passout resale (Wikipedia) vs
  financier's arrest / no money (writewyattuk interview) — BOTH
  PLAUSIBLE, not mutually exclusive.
- B8. Cartel/Rough Trade distribution chronology: 1978 (Crack) vs "later
  emerged" (Wikipedia) — PARTIAL.
- B9. "1970s singles collapse" narrative vs BPI units (89.1m 1979
  record) — RESOLVED: not unit collapse; value/margin and album-spend
  shift.
- B10. Royalty base: 90%-of-retail convention (period anecdote) vs
  dealer-price (PPD) in modern analyses — base for UK majors inferred,
  not documented in 1970s UK primary contract.
- B11. Chart "minimum price" rules: only modern rules found; 1970s
  cut-price ineligibility NOT established — do not assert.
- B12. Royalty range: 8-15% UK vs "5-15% typical" (same overview,
  different blocks); Jam at 6% — new acts at bottom of range.

## E. Resolution merges (resolution-*.md)

  1973 — platinum £1,000,000 / gold £250,000 / silver £75,000 (MW keys
  1974-75); silver raised to £100,000 "as from 1st Jan 76"; gold
  £300,000 / silver £150,000 for releases on/after 1 Jan 1977; unit
  thresholds (300k/100k/60k) appear in keys "as of Jan '79" (first

  manufacturer revenue) still contested.
