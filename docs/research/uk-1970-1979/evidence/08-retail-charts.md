## Dimension 8: Retail and chart economics

### Summary
- Retail anchors are thin but usable: UK 45s recalled at ~45p and LPs
  ~£2.50 in 1974, LP ~£2.99 by 1977 (collector recollection, low
  confidence). US list prices show the mid/late-70s step pattern: LP
  $4.98→$5.98 (1972)→$6.98 (1973)→$7.98 (1976)→$8.98 (1978); 45s
  $0.99→$1.49 (1975).
- BPI ran a retail-VALUE-based album award scheme from 1973: Platinum =
  £1,000,000 at retail; forum account says this was replaced by unit
  thresholds (300,000/100,000/60,000) around Jan 1978 because inflation
  eroded the £ levels. Singles awards were 250,000 (silver) / 500,000
  (gold) / 1,000,000 (platinum) until 1989.
- Chart mechanics: singles chart was a BMRB Top 50 from Feb 1969 and
  expanded to a Top 75 in May 1978; it is compiled from a sample of
  roughly 250 shops sending sales logs by post, so entry thresholds are
  sample estimates, not audited counts.
- Market size: UK vinyl album trade value peaked at £163m in 1978;
  cassettes (MC) quintupled to £252m within ten years and overtook vinyl
  album value by 1986 — model cassette as a late-decade grower.
- Tax: VAT on records ran 10% (from 1 Apr 1973) → 8% (29 Jul 1974) → 8%
  through 1979 → 15% from 1979 (standard rate); pre-1973 purchase tax
  sat on the WHOLESALE price (50% code on 1968 pressings), so tax sits
  between wholesale and retail.
- Biggest gap: no documented label/distributor/shop percentage split of
  retail price found for the 1970s UK; do not model it as fact yet.

### Tensions & counter-arguments
- The BPI album switch from £-value to units is disputed: the forum
  source says the BPI site claims January 1978, but the same poster
  notes Record Mirror continued to quote value-based levels until 1981
  and says he is unsure of the date — treat Jan 1978 as provisional, not
  settled.
- A second UKMix thread reports original 1973 album levels as gold
  200,000 / silver 100,000 units, contradicting the value-based (£1m
  platinum) account; I could not find an official BPI archival page
  resolving this. Both cannot be right as stated.
- VAT detail sources disagree on the higher rate: the House of Lords
  Library table shows 25% for 1974-76 and 12.5% for 1976-79, while other
  summaries describe a 12.5% higher rate introduced in July 1974. The
  standard-rate path (10% → 8% → 15%) is consistent across sources.
- Price anchors come from collector memory: an anecdote gives ~£2.99 per
  LP in 1977, while another recollection says UK albums were £2.50-£3 in
  1970 rising to ~£4.99 by the end of the decade
  (https://www.quora.com/How-much-did-LPS-cost-in-1970). Official UK
  yearly list prices were not located; US list data shows the step
  pattern but not the UK timing.
- No 1970s evidence found for a chart "minimum price" convention; the
  only minimum-price rules located are modern (OCC rules PDF, 2023;
  Billboard 2008). Do not state 1970s cut-price ineligibility as fact.

### Leads & unresolved
- Label/distributor/shop split of retail price for 1970s UK (the core
  wholesale-chain question): check Music Week / Record Retailer trade
  surveys, BPI yearbooks, and any Monopolies/price-commission style
  reports — nothing located in open web searches so far.
- Official UK list prices by year 1970-79 for singles and albums
  (including price categories like budget/mid-price): catalogue adverts
  or Music Week price pages would settle the 45p/£2.50/£2.99 anecdote
  chain.
- BPI original silver and gold £-value levels for albums (only platinum
  £1m found); the BPI archive or 1970s Music Week award announcements
  should carry them.
- Chart-placement sales thresholds: no copy-count data found for
  entering the Top 50/Top 75; the 250-shop sample and postal returns
  imply estimated national sales — a later pass could look for Alan
  Jones / Music Week chart-analysis columns.
- Import prices, Woolworths/mail-order discounting, and cassette UNITS
  (not just value) were not found; BPI annual report tables 1975-79 are
  the obvious next source.
