## Dimension 4: Record deals and contract structures
### Summary
- Beginner-tier UK majors' signings were cheap in 1977: The Jam got
  £6,000 and a 6% royalty from Polydor, raised to 13% when a
  four-album/four-year option was exercised. The game's £150k+ major
  advances fit only star tiers (Elton John: reported $8m for six albums,
  1974), not new acts.
- Hype-driven new acts could leapfrog peers: same year, The Clash got
  £100,000 (CBS) and the Sex Pistols collected £40,000 (EMI 1976), then
  £75,000+ (A&M 1977), then ~$250,000 (Virgin 1977) — a ~16x spread over
  The Jam. Model manager/negotiation leverage, not a flat new-act price.
- Royalty rates cluster 5-15% period-wide (UK 8-15%); the base was
  conventionally computed on ~90% of the retail price. The game's 10-15%
  major band is in range; nothing found supports 25%+ rates for new UK
  acts (small-label terms untested this pass).
- Structural norms: label commits 1 album and holds options toward ~3
  years/6 albums ('1+5'), masters stay with the label, and ~25% of the
  artist's publishing income can be locked down; writers commonly
  assigned copyright for full term on a 50:50 publisher split.
- Recoupment: advances are repaid from the artist's royalty share (not
  label gross); recording costs, video, tour support and some promotion
  are recoupable, and the advance often doubles as the recording budget
  — headline figures are rarely take-home cash. Challenges were real:
  Schroeder v Macaulay (HL 1974) voided an onerous publishing deal.
- No 1970s UK source found for management commission %; only modern
  guidance (15-25% of gross) — treat as a design default, not period
  evidence.

### Tensions & counter-arguments
- Same deal, different numbers: the Pistols' A&M payout is "at least
  £75,000" in one retelling and "nearer £125,000" including severance.
  Treat period deal amounts as reported ranges and labels' spin, not
  precise accounting.
- Famous-act inflation vs beginner reality: Pistols/Clash/Elton dominate
  coverage, but the only clean beginner anchor found is The Jam
  (£6,000/6%, 1977). The Jam row is deliberately left unflagged for the
  famous-act rule because the datapoint comes from the band's unknown
  stage; the celebrated deals are flagged in the table.
- The game's assumed major advances (£150k-£2.25M) exceed every 1977 UK
  new-act figure found; even the 1974 star deal was a reported contract
  value ($8m for six albums — roughly $1.3m per album, derived, not
  necessarily cash advances).
- Royalty base conflict: a period source supports a 90%-of-retail base,
  while modern contract analyses describe dealer-price (PPD) bases; no
  1977 UK primary contract was seen, so the base for UK majors remains
  inferred.
- Rate range conflict: one overview gives 8-15% (UK) while the 1970s
  block of the same piece says the typical artist royalty stuck between
  5-15%; the Jam started at 6% — new acts sit at the bottom of the
  range.
### Leads & unresolved
- Producer royalty 'points' for 1970s UK: no rate found. Shel Talmy
  confirms independent producers took royalties ("You pay me, but I get
  royalties") but no figure; check producer contracts/biographies
  (Talmy, Visconti, Mickie Most) and trade press.
- Management commission % in the 1970s: only modern guidance found (ISM:
  managers 15-25% of gross; US sources 15-20%); check band biographies,
  MMF/ISM archives.
- Publishing advances and sub-publishing terms: not verified; check
  Music Week/Melody Maker archives and the later chapters of the gov.uk
  2023 report.
- Small/indie label economics (Stiff, Chiswick, Rough Trade 1977-79):
  untested; needed to check the game's small-label tier (25-50%
  royalties, £0-£25k advances).
- Clash deal term reported elsewhere as long-term (13 albums) and a
  Pistols "two-year" EMI term seen only via social media — verify both
  against contemporary trade press.
- Further 1970s contract disputes (e.g., the Badfinger/Stan Polley
  management case; artistic-control battles) flagged but not verified
  this pass.
