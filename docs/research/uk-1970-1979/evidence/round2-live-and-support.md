## Round 2: Unknown-tier live income and support slots

### Verdicts
- Target 1: Partially resolved — a documented per-band pub/social-club
  fee band exists (£25–£45 for a small local band, from Finnegan's
  Milton Keynes fieldwork), but its fieldwork window is c.1980–84, not
  the 1970s; the only strictly-1970s figure found is a solo pianist's
  25s a night in a club booking advertisement, so the 1970–79
  unknown-band number is still not nailed.
- Target 2: Unresolved — no fee, flat-expenses or petrol-money figure
  for a 1970s UK support slot was located in any source opened this
  pass; the only support-slot money evidence is a promoter-side remark
  that there "wouldn't be much money", which is not a term.

### What this changes for the game
- The beginner band's realistic pub fee is per band, not per musician:
  £25–£45 split four ways is £6–£11 a head, which is beer money against
  a MU union-scale night. Model the fee as a band-level lump that the
  band divides.
- A solo club residency (25s a night) is the true floor. Any band fee
  should read as a multiple of that, which makes the jump from solo to
  four-piece feel like a real economic step up.
- Transport, repairs and PA are not inside the fee. Both the Finnegan
  and Birch material point to costs eating the fee, so a beginner's
  weekly net should routinely be negative without day jobs.
- Support slots should be modelled as unpaid exposure plus, at best,
  expenses. Nothing found supports a support fee in the 1970s, so paying
  one would be a fabrication.
- Still guesswork: the exact 1970–79 pub fee. The £25–£45 figure is a
  c.1980–84 price and should not be silently backdated; if used for
  1975, deflate it explicitly and label it an estimate.

### Still unresolved
- The 1970–79 pub fee itself. Tried Internet Archive full-text search on
  Will Birch, Jon Savage, Wilko Johnson, Heylin, Finnegan and Taylor;
  archive.org's lending restrictions blocked full download, so only
  snippet-level retrieval was possible. The specific archive that would
  settle it: the Musicians' Union rate cards and branch minutes
  deposited at the University of Stirling, plus the MU's own journal,
  The Musician, for 1970–79.
- Working men's club concert secretaries' booking rates. Taylor (1972)
  reproduces advertisements but the book stops at 1972. The Working
  Men's Club and Institute Union archive, and Club Mirror issues for
  1970–79, would carry per-night artiste rates.
- Support-slot terms. No document found. The NME and Sounds classified
  sections for 1976–79, and the Bishopsgate Institute punk archive's gig
  flyers and correspondence, are the places a "fee" or "expenses only"
  line would appear.
- Per-musician versus per-band convention for the 1970s. Finnegan is
  explicit that her figure is per band; no 1970s source confirmed the
  convention, and MU scales were per musician, so the two conventions
  coexisted.
