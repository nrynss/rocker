## Round 2: The wholesale chain and the price gaps

Research date: 2026-10-30. All Music Week PDFs read directly from
worldradiohistory.com; page numbers are PDF page numbers.

### Verdicts

- **Wholesale chain: PARTIALLY RESOLVED.** The retail end of the chain
  is now documented with real, mutually consistent 1979 numbers: a
  dealer price, a retail price and a named margin percentage that
  reconcile to the penny (Island, Nov 1979: £5.00 retail / £2.61 dealer
  ex-VAT / 40% margin). The standard trade term is also documented: a
  "full 36⅔ per cent margin" on sale-or-return (Music Week, Apr 1979).
  **What remains unresolved is the split *inside* the wholesale price**
  — no 1970s UK source found that divides the manufacturer/label share
  from the distributor's or wholesaler's share. Every "distributor's
  cut" figure searched for is absent; the sources document the
  *retailer's* cut precisely and leave the rest as one undifferentiated
  block.
- **Price gaps: 2 of 4 filled, 1 partially, 1 not.** 1979 singles filled
  (£1.01 Polydor RRP, Aug 1979). Nov 1975 LP average filled (£2.85,
  correcting the Oct £2.89) — but this is a 1975 figure published in a
  1976 issue, so **1976 itself remains unfilled**. 1978 singles: not
  found. 1971–72: **unfillable from this archive** — worldradiohistory
  holds no 1970–72 Music Week/Record Retailer issues at all (see Still
  unresolved).

### A split-of-retail-price model, as far as evidence allows

**Case A — one real release, all three numbers documented (Island,
*Broken English*, Nov 1979).** VAT 15% from 1979.

```
Retail (RRP, incl. VAT 15%)                     £5.00    [documented]
  less VAT (5.00 - 5.00/1.15)                   £0.65
  = net retail ex-VAT                           £4.35

Retailer pays dealer price (ex-VAT)             £2.61    [documented]
  dealer price incl. VAT (2.61 x 1.15)          £3.00
Retailer gross margin = 5.00 - 3.00             £2.00    [documented as "40 per cent"]
  2.00 / 5.00                                   = 40.0%  [checks exactly]

Of the £2.61 the retailer pays:
  manufacturer/label share                      ?????    [NOT documented]
  distributor's share                           ?????    [NOT documented]
  ---------------------------------------------
  total                                          £2.61   [documented only as a total]
```

The 40% is computed on the **VAT-inclusive** RRP, not on net-of-VAT
revenue — that is why £2.61 × 1.15 = £3.00 and £5.00 − £3.00 = 40.0%
exactly. This matters for the game: a "40% margin" in 1970s UK trade
usage means *retailer keeps 40p of every £1 on the ticket*, and the
state's VAT is inside that £1.

**Case B — the standard term, arithmetic derived (not a second
source).** The "full 36⅔ per cent margin" of Apr 1979, applied to the
same £5.00 ticket:

```
Retail (RRP, incl. VAT)                         £5.00    [rate documented]
  retailer keeps 36.667%                        £1.83    [derived]
  retailer pays 63.333%                         £3.17    [derived]
    of which VAT (3.17 - 3.17/1.15)             £0.41
    net to label + distributor                  £2.75    [derived]
```

So the documented range on a top-price 1979 LP is: retailer takes **36⅔%
(normal) to 40% (exceptional)**, and the label-plus-distribution block
takes the remaining **60–63⅓%** of the ticket.

**Where the chain stops being visible.** The £2.61 is a single
undifferentiated sum. Nothing found in 1971–79 splits it between the
label and whoever physically distributed. The only structural evidence
that distribution was a separately paid function is qualitative: in the
same Nov 1979 issue Island drops EMI and moves to "independent
distributors, including Terry Blood, Lightning, Lugton's, One-Stop and
Wynd-Up" — a list of five *distinct* distribution firms, which proves
the tier existed as a business but prices none of it. Likewise Apr 1979
shows EMI's LRD acting as a licensed distributor for Motown, Island,
Stiff, RAK, MCA and others, with the 36⅔% margin as the retailer-facing
term — but LRD's own remuneration from those labels is never stated.

**Estimate, clearly labelled as such:** if a distributor in this period
took a conventional 10–15% of the wholesale price, the label would net
roughly £2.22–£2.35 of the £2.61 on Case A. **This is my inference, not
a source.** The game should hold the £2.61 and the 40% as hard numbers
and treat the internal split as a tunable.

### Still unresolved

- **The distributor's/wholesaler's percentage.** No 1970s UK figure
  found. Named archives that would settle it: the Monopolies and Mergers
  Commission report on the supply of records (there was an MMC/Price
  Commission look at records in the late 1970s); BPI Yearbook editions
  1975–1980 (holdings: British Library, Barbican); the Gramophone Record
  Retailers Committee (GRRC) papers, which were actively negotiating
  these margins in Aug 1979.
- **1971–72 prices: not fillable from this archive.**
  worldradiohistory's Music Week index lists **zero issues for 1970,
  1971 and 1972** (coverage jumps from two 1969 *Record Retailer* issues
  to 1973). Record Retailer was the title until it relaunched as Music
  Week on 18 March 1972, so 1971–72 pricing must come from a different
  holding: the British Library's trade-paper collection, or Billboard's
  UK correspondence pages for 1971–72.
- **1976 prices.** The Jan 1976 issue carries the *Nov 1975* index; the
  Jul 1976 and Dec 1976 issues carry no index at all. **The Record Price
  Index appears to have lapsed between early 1976 and 1977** — it is
  absent from every 1977 issue sampled (Jan, Feb, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep,
  Oct, Nov, Dec), both 1978 issues sampled, the Jun 1978 issue, and all
  four 1979 issues. If the game's timeline needs 1976–79 *averages*,
  they must be reconstructed from list prices, not read from an index.
- **1978 singles RRP.** Not found. The Jun 1978 issue sampled has no
  price story; the 1979 £1.01 figure would place 1978 somewhere in the
  90p–£1.01 band, but that is inference, not evidence.
- **Whether the 36⅔% margin is quoted on VAT-inclusive or exclusive
  RRP** is *not* stated at the point of use; the Island case, where the
  arithmetic only closes on a VAT-inclusive base, is the reason I have
  modelled it that way. A single confirming sentence from a BPI circular
  would remove the assumption.
