# Round 2: The cost of getting noticed

Scope: UK, 1970s. Promotion/promotion-adjacent COSTS only. Live fees,
deal terms, retail splits, equipment and representation percentages are
out of scope (sibling briefs).

Method: primary scans of the trade and music press, read as text. Rate
cards are printed *inside* the papers, so they are quoted from the paper
itself rather than from a secondary summary.

---

### Verdicts

- **Ad rates — Partially resolved.** Classified and semi-display rates
  are now documented with year, unit and source (Music Week 1976/77;
  Sounds 1977). The big-ticket number a label actually cared about — the
  **display page rate** in NME / Melody Maker / Sounds — was **not**
  found in any issue I could open. No display rate is asserted below.
- **Publicist / plugger — Unresolved.** No period fee, retainer or
  per-campaign figure was located in the 1973–77 Music Week issues read.
  Only modern (2020s) plugger pricing surfaced, which is useless for a
  1970s game and is deliberately excluded. What *is* documented is that
  pluggers existed as a paid intermediary role and that chart-rigging by
  pluggers was alleged in the trade press — the allegation, not a
  tariff.
- **Posters / handbills / listings — Partially resolved.** The *listing
  ad* is priced (it is a classified, so it is priced like one: per
  word). Unit costs for printed posters and handbills were not found in
  the issues read.
- **Demo duplication — Partially resolved.** Studio time and tape
  copying/editing rates are documented for 1976; a standalone
  acetate/demo-pressing price list was not found.

Read plainly: the *small* end of promotion is cheap and fully priced — a
few pounds buys a listing, £2 buys a trade ad, £6.50 buys a column inch
in the industry's own paper. The *expensive* end (a display page, a
plugger, a publicist) is exactly where the sources went quiet. That
silence is itself a finding about where the money actually went.

### Still unresolved

- **Display (page) rates for NME, Melody Maker, Sounds, Record Mirror.**
  Classified/semi-display is priced; the full display card is not
  printed in the issues I opened. Settle at: British Library **Newspaper
  Press Directory** or **Advertiser's Annual** for 1972–1979 (rate
  tables by title), or IPC/Spotlight's own media packs in the **History
  of Advertising Trust** (HAT) archive.
- **Press officer / plugger retainers.** Music Week 1973–77 carries the
  *culture* but no tariff. Settle at: **Music Week** 1974–1978 issues
  via the same archive (query "plugging company", "publicity", "press
  officer" — the archive's search.cgi returns issue filenames, a fast
  index), or incorporated-plugger company accounts at **Companies
  House** (fees appear as turnover per client).
- **Poster / handbill unit costs.** Not in the music press. Settle at:
  period **printers' price lists** in local trade directories, or punk
  flyer printing invoices held in the **Punk Archive** / **John Savage
  collection**; the Stiff and Chiswick memoirs name figures anecdotally.
- **Payola.** No payment was documented in anything read; only an
  allegation in a 1976 column. Settle at: **IBA/Capital Radio**
  corporate records and the **Broadcasting** trade press for 1973–76,
  and the IBA's annual reports on advertising standards.
