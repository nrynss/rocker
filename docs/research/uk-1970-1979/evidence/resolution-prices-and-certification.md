# Resolution 2 — Retail prices and certification thresholds

Prepared 16 Oct 2026. All page numbers are PDF pages as extracted from
worldradiohistory.com scans of *Music Week*; the OCR in these scans
mangles punctuation, so quotes are lightly normalised (noted where
material). Prices are as printed by the source; no price below is
invented or reconstructed.

## Target 1 — UK retail prices by year

**Verdict: Partially resolved.** Contemporary anchors now pin singles
from Dec 1970 to Apr 1977 and full-price LPs from Jul 1973 to Jan 1980.
Gaps: 1971–72 (nothing located), 1976 (no list-price item found), and
1978–79 *single* prices (award symbols found, no retail price).

### The recollections, adjudicated
- **"45p singles / £2.50 LPs in 1974"**: as *list prices*, inconsistent
  with the trade evidence — CBS had raised singles to 50p by **Dec
  1970** and full-price LPs printed at £2.42–£2.45 in Jul 1973/Nov 1974.
  Could describe discounted stock or an earlier year; not defensible as
  1974 list prices.
- **"LP £2.99 in 1977"**: matches a *compilation* price (£2.99, Feb
  1977) and the BBC's new budget category (£2.50, Apr 1977) — not the
  major-label full-price level (£3.49 RRP).
- **"£2.50–£3.00 in 1970 rising to ~£4.99 by 1979"**: the £4.99 endpoint
  matches product priced in late 1979 (£4.99, Jan 1980 item); the 1970
  start is unsupported (no 1970–72 LP price located).
- The recollections collide only when list and discounted/compilation
  tiers are conflated; they reconcile once the tiers are separated.
  (`in2013dollars.com` inflation pages — fetched earlier this run: 1974,
  1977, 1979 — are usable only as context, e.g. £100 of 1974 ≈ £1,352
  later.)

### VAT treatment and what "list price" meant
- The *Music Week* **Record Price Index** (6 Dec 1975, p.60) states its
  method: figures "calculated from the published price lists of eighteen
  major UK record companies" (A&M, Anchor, Trojan, Bell, CBS, Decca,
  DJM, EMI, Phonogram, Polydor, President, Pye, RCA, Transatlantic,
  United Artists, Virgin, WEA named on the page), for full-price
  product, RRP **incl. VAT**; it tracks month-on-month movement (Oct vs
  Sept 1975) and notes which company announced rises since the last
  index ("Decca only").
- Era VAT context: 10% from Apr 1973, 8% from Jul 1974, 15% from 1979 —
  so the Oct 1975 anchor sits under 8%, and pre-1973 prices are not
  VAT-inclusive in the same sense.
- "List price" = manufacturer's RRP; transaction reality diverged: 80p
  bulk mail-order LPs (Sep 1974, VAT shown separately), "full 36⅓%
  dealer margin" (Dec 1975), dealer lists marked "All prices inc.
  Royalty, add VAT" (Feb 1978), and 75p-off voucher promotions (Feb
  1977).
- Chart convention: the *Music Week* LP chart header carries a
  price-scope line — "[LPs] retailing at £1.49 and upwards" (6 Dec 1975,
  p.63) — i.e. cheaper LPs sat outside the LP chart. Relevant if the
  game models price-based chart eligibility.

## Target 2 — BPI award thresholds and dates

**Verdict: Resolved on the central contradiction; partially resolved in
fine print.** Contemporaneous *Music Week* chart keys show **album
awards were value-based (£) from Apr 1973 through sales year 1978**,
with **unit thresholds effective "as of Jan '79"**; **singles were
unit-based (250,000/500,000/1,000,000) throughout**. The competing
account that 1973 album levels were already units (gold 200,000 / silver
100,000) finds no support in any fetched contemporary source.

### Best-supported timeline
- **Apr 1973 — scheme introduced** (new releases only). UKMix post #1,
  quoting bpi.co.uk: "the BPI began certifying sales in the UK in April
  1973, and multi-platinum awards were introduced in February 1987";
  post #3: singles released before Apr 1973 do not qualify unless
  re-released after 01/04/73.
  https://www.ukmix.org/forum/chart-discussion/chart-analysis/34071-bpi-sales-awards-history
  — Corroborating context (earlier pass): BPI's 2018 relaunch copy dates
  the scheme to 1973 and names Elton John's *Goodbye Yellow Brick Road*
  the first gold album (1 Oct 1973):
  https://www.recordoftheday.com/news-and-press/platinum-gold-silver-discs-are-relaunched-as-brit-certified-awards
- **1974–75 keys (verified verbatim).** "PLATINUM LP (£1 million sales)
  / GOLD LP (£250,000 sales, LPs released 1st Sept '74) / SILVER LP
  (£75,000 sales)" — MW 6 Dec 1975, p.63; MW 27 Dec 1975, p.23. BPI
  silver ran on £-value sales: Island applied for a BPI silver disc on
  ~£100,000 advance sales (MW 9 Nov 1974, p.2).
- **From 1 Jan 1976 — silver raised to £100,000.** Key reads "SILVER LP
  (£100,000 sales as from 1st Jan. 76)" — MW 22 May 1976, p.45; MW 25
  Dec 1976, p.23. (This contradicts Wikipedia's "Jan 1975" date for the
  £100k level; the trade key wins.)
- **From 1 Jan 1977 (releases) — gold £300,000 / silver £150,000.**
  "GOLD LP (£300,000 on or after 1st Jan. '77) / SILVER LP (£150,000 on
  or after 1st Jan. '77)" — MW 19 Feb 1977 p.63; 30 Apr 1977 p.55; 24
  Dec 1977 p.47; 21 Jan 1978 p.109; 11 Feb 1978 p.63; 16 Dec 1978 p.59;
  **20 Jan 1979 p.101 (last monetary key found)**. Practice matches: a
  silver disc is reported "with sales of over £150,000" (MW 17 Feb 1979,
  p.38).
- **Jan 1979 — album unit thresholds take effect.** Keys switch to
  "PLATINUM LP (300,000 units as of Jan '79) / GOLD LP (100,000 units as
  of Jan '79) / SILVER LP (60,000 units as of Jan '79)" — first verified
  appearance MW 2 Jun 1979, p.55; also 14 Jul 1979 p.55; 15 Dec 1979
  p.34; 12 Jan 1980 p.36. Applied in practice by Dec 1979: "Diana Ross
  *20 Golden Greats* has sold over 300,000 copies and won itself a
  platinum award" (MW 15 Dec 1979, p.36). Transition window: between the
  20 Jan 1979 and 2 Jun 1979 issues; the key itself dates the new levels
  "as of Jan '79".
- **Singles — unit thresholds throughout: 250,000 silver / 500,000 gold
  / 1,000,000 platinum.** Contemporaneous: singles forecast keys
  "MILLION (PLATINUM) / ½ MILLION (GOLD) / ¼ MILLION (SILVER)" (MW 22
  May 1976 p.55; 30 Apr 1977 p.57; 21 Jan 1978 p.111; 11 Feb 1978 p.65 —
  OCR renders the fractions imperfectly); "silver disc (250,000 sales) …
  gold disc (500,000 sales)" (MW 15 Dec 1979, p.9); silver disc for
  "sales in excess of 250,000 copies … of the single" (MW 16 Dec 1978,
  p.3).
- **On/after 1 Jan 1989 — singles thresholds lowered** to
  200,000/400,000/600,000 for records released from that date (UKMix
  post #3; current levels per post #1). **Multi-platinum introduced Feb
  1987** (UKMix post #1).

### What remains contested and why
1. **Accounting basis of the value thresholds.** *Music Week* prints
   "£250,000 sales" / "£1,000,000 sales"; a UKMix poster (recalling
   BPI-site wording) says "at retail value"; a search snippet attributed
   to the same thread (later pages, not fetchable this run) says
   "revenue received by manufacturers". Retail vs
   trade/manufacturer-revenue are different bases — do **not** convert £
   thresholds to units without stating basis. (Arithmetically,
   £1,000,000 ÷ £2.89 avg retail ≈ 346,000 LPs, close to the 300,000
   level later adopted, but the derivation is undocumented.)
2. **The "abolished January 1978" claim.** UKMix post #3 reports the BPI
   site as saying the monetary system was abolished in **January 1978**;
   but the 1978 keys and the Jan 1979 key are still monetary, and unit
   wording appears "as of Jan '79" by Jun 1979. The snippet "Between 1st
   April 1973 and 31st December 1978 …" is consistent with a change
   applying to **1979**; the 1978 recollection appears off by one year.
   The BPI's own 1978–79 announcement was not located (live and archived
   bpi.co.uk are scripted shells).
3. **Early value levels (1973–74).** Only the Sep-1974 breakpoint ("LPs
   released 1st Sept '74") is contemporaneously verified; the figures
   gold £150,000 / silver £75,000 as the original Apr-1973 levels rest
   on Wikipedia
   (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Phonographic_Industry). Minor
   tension: Island's silver application on ~£100,000 advance sales (Nov
   1974) vs the £75,000 key of Dec 1975 — unreconciled (basis or
   vintage).
4. **Silver change date.** Wikipedia says Jan 1975 for the £100k level;
   trade keys say "as from 1st Jan. 76". A silver disc presented 31 Jan
   1976 for £75,000 sales (MW 31 Jan 1976, p.1) sits awkwardly with the
   1 Jan 1976 change (possibly a pre-change qualification).
   Best-supported: **1 Jan 1976**.
5. **The "1973 album levels were already units (gold 200,000 / silver
   100,000)" account.** No contemporary support found. Most likely a
   conflation of parallel award systems of the period: *Music Week*'s
   own "MW Gold/Silver LP Award" symbols (MW 27 Dec 1975, p.7),
   label-internal discs (CBS silver discs for "over £75,000" sales, MW
   30 Apr 1977, p.24), and the pre-1973 DISC awards (Silver 250,000
   units / Gold 1,000,000 units, run since 1959, per UKMix post #6) —
   plus later levels (album 60k/100k/300k from Jan 1979; singles
   200k/400k/600k from 1989).
6. **Platinum single = 1,000,000 pre-1989** rests on forum recall (UKMix
   posts); 250k/500k are primary-verified, 1,000,000 is not, this run.
