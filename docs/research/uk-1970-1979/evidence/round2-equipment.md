## Round 2: Instruments, vans and rehearsal space

Scope: UK 1970–1979. New vs secondhand stated on every line. All prices
original currency, as printed.

Method note: the whole 1970s run of *Beat Instrumental* is scanned and
OCR'd at worldradiohistory.com, but Cloudflare blocks direct PDF fetches
(HTTP 429 + challenge) and the `fetch_content` tool truncates PDF text
at ~18k chars (a 80-page issue is ~255k chars). Working path:
`https://r.jina.ai/<pdf-url>` returns the full text layer as markdown,
then `grep -n "£"`. The OCR has no page markers, so citations give issue
+ advertiser + the exact searchable string rather than a page number.

### Verdicts

- **Instruments: Resolved.** Four independent period dealer price lists
  (1971, 1973, 1975 ×2) plus 1977 classified adverts, covering new and
  secondhand guitar, bass, drums, combos and PA. Confidence: high.
- **Van/transport: Partially.** Fuel is nailed to an official
  parliamentary series (1970–1978). A new Transit list price exists for
  1965 only. No 1970s used-van price was found after four distinct
  strategies. Insurance: nothing.
- **Rehearsal space: Resolved (thin).** Two hard period rates — London
  1971 and provincial 1973. Confidence: medium-high on the numbers, low
  on typicality (n=2).

**Derived starter-rig basket** — arithmetic on the documented prices
above, mixing years (1973–1975), clearly an estimate, not a period
quotation:

A four-piece's gigging rig bought secondhand-upgrade in 1975: two
guitars (£45 copy + £160 S/H Tele) + bass (£46) + S/H Rogers kit (£170)
+ two 100W amps (~£196) + PA (£71) ≈ **£688**, or **12.4 weeks** of
average manual wages. Buying the same rig *entirely new but
budget-grade* (Avon copies at £45 each, £46 bass, Shaftesbury-class kit
~£123, new 100W amps, £71 PA) comes to ≈ **£526**, or **9.4 weeks** —
**cheaper** than the mixed basket. The counter-intuitive result worth
designing around: in 1975 new budget gear undercut used professional
gear, so a poor band bought new copies while a band with gig money
bought old Fenders and Rogers. The upward move was not 'new instead of
used' but 'used pro instead of new cheap'.

### Still unresolved

- **Used Transit-type van, 1970s, actual price.** Four strategies
  failed: general web search (returns modern auction results and
  restored-vehicle prices, ~£6k–£28k, useless for period), targeted
  phrase searches on price bands, a search for period newspaper
  classified scans, and grepping all four OCR'd *Beat Instrumental*
  issues for vehicle adverts (the magazine carries instrument
  classifieds only — no vehicle section). The one 1975 Transit mention
  is a touring anecdote with no figure.
- **What would settle it:** *Exchange & Mart* 1970–1979 (the standard UK
  venue for used vehicle private sales) — not digitised publicly.
  *Commercial Motor* classified section, 1970–1979 — the British
  Commercial Vehicle Museum holds runs; some volumes are on archive.org
  but my searches surfaced none. Local paper classified pages via the
  British Newspaper Archive (paywalled, but a free-search snippet will
  confirm whether a "Transit" classified exists in a given year before
  paying).
- **Van insurance and road tax for a young driver, 1970s.** Zero
  results. Motor insurance in this era was rated by vehicle group and
  driver age/record; a band van driven by a 19-year-old would have been
  expensive relative to the vehicle. **What would settle it:** *Which?*
  car-cost surveys (they published annual running-cost tables from the
  early 1970s), or the AA/Motorists' Guide annual. Neither is online in
  full.
- **Typicality of the two rehearsal rates.** £4/hr London (1971) and
  75p/hr Paignton (1973) are both *advertised* rates from studio ads,
  which skew to the professional end. A band renting a room above a pub,
  or a council youth-club room, would have paid less or nothing. No
  source found for the informal end of the market.
- **PA hire as an alternative to buying.** A 1977 advert offers
  "200w–2000w PAs from £10 per day" and "Amps and Cabs from £2 per day"
  (Simon King Music, Sept 1977) — one datapoint on hire, suggesting a
  band could rent a PA for a gig for less than 1/7 of buying one. Worth
  a dedicated pass if the game models hire-vs-buy.
- **Cymbals.** Both drum-kit price lists explicitly exclude cymbals
  (£29–£348 kits, "cymbals extra"). Cymbal prices appear in the 1977
  list only as Zildjian stockist references without figures. A kit price
  is therefore not a playable-kit price.

### Source-access note for round 3

`worldradiohistory.com` **cannot** be curl'd directly — Cloudflare
returns 429 with a JS challenge on both index pages and PDFs. The index
pages *do* work via `curl` for `Beat-Instrumental-UK.htm` (it served the
full file list), but the PDFs do not. Reliable route:

```
curl -sL --max-time 120 "https://r.jina.ai/https://www.worldradiohistory.com/UK/Beat-Instrumental/Beat-Instrumental-1975-04-S-OCR.pdf" -o out.txt
grep -n "£" out.txt
```

Full 1970s coverage exists for *Beat Instrumental* (every month
1970–1979, all `-S-OCR`). Dealer adverts with full price lists cluster
in the front and back thirds of each issue; the classifieds sit at the
very end and yield private secondhand prices. Roughly 15 minutes of wall
time per issue via the proxy.
