## Dimension 3: Studio, recording and manufacturing costs

### Summary
- A self-financed punk single in 1977-78 was a low-hundreds-of-pounds
  project: Desperate Bicycles spent £153 for 500 copies all-in (3 hours
  studio, pressing, sleeves); Scritti Politti's sleeve-printed accounts
  for 2,500 copies total about £515 (recording £98, mastering £40,
  pressing £369.36, labels £8) and even that excluded sleeve printing.
- Sessions were short: 5 hours for the Buzzcocks EP (3 recording + 2
  mixing, 16-track), 14 hours for Scritti Politti. A provincial 16-track
  could sell a 16-hour day for ~£100 (c.1976-77), against London's
  ~£30/hour 16-track rate — a two-tier market the game should model.
- Manufacturing was priced per unit, not per order: Pye pressed at 13p
  per 7-inch copy with £27 plating on a 2,500 run; a lacquer cut cost
  £40, and the Desperate Bicycles pressed the same tracks on both sides
  specifically to avoid paying for a second cut.
- A whole debut EP (~1,000 copies) cost roughly £750 self-financed
  (Buzzcocks, 1976-77), and DIY singles recouped fast: the first
  Desperate Bicycles 500 sold out in four months at £210 profit, the
  next 1,000 in a fortnight.
- Tape is a separate cash trap: Iron Maiden could not afford the £50
  2-inch master tape after their session and the studio wiped it — a
  natural fail state for the game.
- The professional tier is a different universe: Dire Straits' 1978
  debut album cost £12,500 for three weeks at Basing Street including
  artwork (famous act, label-financed); blend this with DIY singles only
  via tiered budgets, and note the given context that cheap pub-rock
  recordings could break even near 2,000 records vs ~20,000 for
  mainstream rock albums.

### Tensions & counter-arguments
- Published cost lists are incomplete and not comparable: Scritti
  Politti's sleeve list excludes sleeve printing (its later EP says
  inserts were "printed cheap"), so the true all-in cost of their
  2,500-copy EP exceeds the ~£515 of listed items; the Desperate
  Bicycles' £153 by contrast bundles sleeves in. Compare like for like
  before balancing the game.
- The DIY rhetoric is contested: the peer-reviewed account (cadernosaa)
  argues "it was easy, it was cheap" still depended on professional
  cutters, pressing plants and printers — and the Desperate Bicycles'
  double-sided pressing was itself a cost compromise, "due to the
  proscriptive cost of cutting a master for two separate sides".
- Studio "rates" are not a single national price: Kemp's London 16-track
  figure (~£30/hour, c.1976-77) versus Scritti Politti's Spaceward
  package (~£7/hour equivalent, 1978) is a 4x spread driven by
  self-built provincial facilities — any single rate in the game is a
  modelling choice.
- Spiral Scratch's accounting lacks granularity: Wikipedia gives ~£750
  total for production and manufacture; the academic article adds a £250
  loan from Pete Shelley's father and that manager Boon arranged
  pressing at Phonogram — neither splits studio from manufacturing cost,
  so per-stage costs for that EP stay unverified.
- Famous-act contrast: Dire Straits' £12,500 album (1978) and the given
  label break-even of ~20,000 records for mainstream rock versus ~2,000
  for cheap recordings imply label-financed albums and self-financed
  singles sit in different cost universes; don't fit one curve through
  both.

### Leads & unresolved
- No verified 1970-79 UK trade-press rate card by track count
  (8/16/24-track). A March 1970 Record Retailer UK studio guide surfaced
  only as a Facebook group post that could not be opened; studio time
  should be checked in Studio Sound, Record Retailer and Melody Maker
  adverts of 1970-79.
- UK pressing-plant turnaround times and minimum orders (Pye Mitcham,
  Orlake, EMI Hayes) were not verified; trade press is the likely source
  — the 13p/copy at a 2,500 run is a unit price, not a turnaround
  promise.
- Undertones' Teenage Kicks recording cost is commonly cited around £200
  for the Belfast Wizard Studios session, but this run only produced a
  Facebook post, not a primary source; check Terri Hooley's memoir and
  1978-79 press.
- Sleeve/artwork unit costs are only found bundled (DB's £153 included
  sleeves; Dire Straits' budget "including artwork"); a separate
  sleeve-printing price list for 7-inch and 12-inch in the 1970s is
  still missing.
- End-of-decade cassette/home recording: no costed data located
  (portastudio-era gear or blank tape prices); late-70s magazine adverts
  are the place to look.
- Rate trend cross-check outside the window: a Studio Focus piece
  (muzines.co.uk, Dec 1983) lists a 14-hour 24-track day at about £300
  (https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/studio-focus/2782) — consistent
  with post-1979 rate inflation; a 1979 datapoint would bridge the two
  eras.
