# Rocker — Unresolved Questions and Contradictions (UK 1970–1979)

Items this pass could **not** settle from public sources, ordered by
impact on the game. Each lists what was tried and what would close it.
Finding references (F-numbers) point into `findings.xlsx`.

---

## Part 1 — Open questions (by priority)

### 1. Unknown-band gig fees in pubs/clubs (HIGH — the core beginner economy is unpriced)
No public source found giving a per-night figure for an unknown UK band
1970–79. Every money datapoint located is for established acts (10cc
£1,000/gig, F03). Closest period data is Musicians' Union non-live rates
(F08). A c.1980 social post claiming a £40 Hope & Anchor fee was
unverifiable. **Tried:** MU history chapter; targeted searches on Hope &
Anchor / Canvey / pub-rock memoirs; Will Birch's Loudersound excerpt (no
figures); working-men's-club fee searches. **To close:** British
Newspaper Archive scans of Melody Maker/NME "gig wanted" and agency ads
1971–79; Will Birch's *No Sleep Till Canvey Island* full text; MU
deposited records (Stirling University); EThOS/ProQuest theses on 1970s
live music economics.

### 2. Support-slot terms — paid, or expenses only? (HIGH)
The gigs are documented (Sex Pistols supporting Eddie and the Hot Rods,
Marquee, Feb 1976) but no source states the money terms. Game design
currently uses an explicit inference (report.md E4). **To close:** Jon
Savage's *England's Dreaming* first-gigs chapters; Hot Rods/promoter
interviews (Rocksbackpages, paywalled).

### 3. Booking-agent commission, 1970s UK (HIGH — agency is a planned feature)
Only modern guidance found (10–25%; 10–15% indie agents) — must not be
backdated. **To close:** Music Week/Melody Maker agency display ads (may
state commission); NEMS/Harry Fox agency histories; artist memoirs.

### 4. Management commission %, 1970s UK (MEDIUM — partial data exists, F23)
Best period data is 1960s (Beatles/Epstein: 10–15% of income rising to
25% of gross; "most managers" ~10%) plus one 1975 non-UK datapoint (20%
of gross, AC/DC). No direct UK-1970s percentage. **To close:** Peter
Grant biography; MMF history; Simon Frith, *The Sociology of Rock*
(1978); court filings (e.g. Gilbert O'Sullivan v Gordon Mills, 1980s).

### 5. Producer royalty "points", 1970s UK (MEDIUM)
Genuine gap: no period number for producer points/per-cent deals on
retail or royalties. Qualitative evidence only (independent producers
took royalties from the early 1960s). **To close:** producer biographies
(Visconti, Dudgeon); trade-press deal reports; production contracts.

### 6. Instrument, PA, van and rehearsal-space prices (MEDIUM — early-game equipment economy)
Only one period gear scan found (F10: used Vox amp £45 / cabinet £25,
1970). No van prices, PA prices, drum kits or rehearsal rents for 1970s
UK. **To close:** Beat Instrumental / Melody Maker classified archives;
1970s Yellow Pages; British Library trade directories.

### 7. Label/distributor/shop split of retail price (MEDIUM — the wholesale chain)
No documented 1970s UK percentage split found. Fragmentary signals:
"full 36⅓% dealer margin" (Music Week, Dec 1975); wholesale margin
~$2–3/copy cited in the project's own v0.8 design, but uncited to a
1970s UK source. **To close:** Music Week/Record Retailer trade surveys;
BPI yearbooks; Monopolies Commission-style reports.

### 8. Retail price gaps (LOW–MEDIUM)
Contemporary Music Week anchors cover singles 1970→1977 and LP 1973→1980
(F37); missing: 1971–72 (both), 1976 (both), 1978–79 singles. Collector
recollections conflict with trade list prices and were adjudicated as
tier confusion. **To close:** the Music Week "Record Price Index" across
1973–79; Record Mirror 1981–82; pre-1973 Melody Maker/Billboard ads.

### 9. BPI threshold fine print (LOW — central question resolved, F38)
Resolved: value-based albums Apr 1973–1978 (£1m/£250k/£75k, then
£300k/£150k from Jan 1977), unit thresholds from Jan 1979; singles
unit-based 250k/500k/1m. Still contested: (a) whether £ thresholds were
retail value or manufacturer revenue — do not convert to units; (b)
exact 1979 change announcement (between 20 Jan and 2 Jun 1979 issues);
(c) original Apr-1973 levels (only Sept-1974 breakpoint verified). **To
close:** BPI archive; Music Week Feb–Jun 1979; British Library BPI
files.

### 10. Dole details for the single claimant (MEDIUM — the typical punk case)
Found: family-claimant rates (F09) and eligibility thresholds. Missing:
single man's rate 1976–79; whether gigging while signing on was policed
or deemed fraudulent; declaration rules. **To close:** DHSS
leaflets/annual abstracts; Hansard benefit debates; Musicians' Union
archives; punk oral histories.

### 11. Promotion cost side (MEDIUM)
Found: reach figures (press circulations F26, Peel F24, fanzines F25)
and demo cost (F28). Missing: 1970s music-paper advertising rates, press
agent/publicist fees, flyposting/handbill costs, ILR (Capital/LBC from
1973) playlist power, needle-time quota numbers. **To close:** British
Library trade yearbooks; IBA archives; Stiff/Chiswick press budgets.

### 12. Minor open items
- Spiral Scratch cost accounting discrepancy (~£750 vs ~£500) — F13.
- Sniffin' Glue issue-1 print run (50) vs sales (~300, Christie's lot) —
  F25.
- Pistols A&M payout range (£75k "at least" vs ~£125k incl. severance) —
  F22.
- Schroeder v Macaulay £50: litigated floor, not an average advance — no
  "typical advance" range found for new songwriters (F20).
- Undertones' "Teenage Kicks" cost (~£200 commonly cited) — no primary
  source retrieved.
- 1979 bridging datapoint between Spaceward's ~£100/day (c.1976–77, F14)
  and 1983's ~£300/14-hr day (muzines.co.uk) — noted in F14.
- Whether any proto-indie chart existed in 1979 before the official Jan
  1980 launch (F31).
- Chart entry copy-count thresholds (none exist in sources; the chart is
  a ~250-shop sample, F39).
- Iron Maiden's 1979 self-release run of 5,000 copies sits outside the
  40 selected findings but is in `evidence/03-studio-costs.md`.
