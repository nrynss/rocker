# Rocker — Unresolved Questions and Contradictions (UK 1970–1979)

What the sources still will not settle, ordered by impact on the game,
each with the specific archive that would close it. Finding references
(F-numbers) point into `findings.csv`. Round 1 ran eight dimensions and
three resolution passes; round 2 attacked six clusters of the gaps below
and **closed five of the top six** — the table in Part 3 records what
moved.

---

## Part 1 — Still open (by priority)

### 1. The unknown band's night's pay, 1970–79 (HIGH — still the core gap, but now bounded)
Two rounds have failed to find a strictly-1970s per-night figure for an
unknown band. What round 2 did establish:

- The closest figure is **£25–£45 per band per night** for a small local
  band in pubs (F69) — but Finnegan's fieldwork window is c.1980–84, not
  the 1970s. It is recorded with that caveat and must not be silently
  backdated; deflate it explicitly if used for 1975.
- The absolute floor of club work was a **solo pianist at 25s a night**
  (c.1969–71, F70), which makes a band fee legible as a multiple of a
  lone player's night.
- Pub-tier economics were zero-margin: memoir evidence has pub-rock bands
  surviving on pub work with members "getting on each others pockets"
  (F72).

**Tried:** MU history; Hope & Anchor / Canvey / pub-rock memoir searches;
Internet Archive full-text search across Birch, Savage, Wilko Johnson,
Heylin, Finnegan and Taylor (lending-restricted — snippet level only).
**To close:** Musicians' Union rate cards and branch minutes (University
of Stirling deposit) and the MU journal *The Musician* for 1970–79; the
Working Men's Club and Institute Union archive and *Club Mirror*
1970–79 for concert-secretary rates; NME/Sounds classifieds 1976–79.

### 2. Support-slot money terms (HIGH — two rounds, nothing)
No fee, expenses rate or petrol-money figure for a 1970s UK support slot
exists in any source opened. The only evidence is a promoter-side remark
that there "wouldn't be much money" in it (F71), which is not a term.
The game's current design uses an explicit inference (report.md E4) and
should keep it labelled as such.
**To close:** Jon Savage, *England's Dreaming*, first-gigs chapters;
NME/Sounds classifieds 1976–79; the Bishopsgate Institute punk archive's
gig flyers and correspondence, where a "fee" or "expenses only" line
would appear.

### 3. Producer royalty points, 1970s UK (HIGH — two rounds, nothing numeric)
No 1970s UK producer rate with a named producer, artist, year and a
retail-versus-royalty basis has been found (F50). The qualitative fact is
solid — independent producers took royalties from the early 1960s — but
the size is not. **Do not import modern 2–4 points**, which would silently
bring 1990s practice into a 1970s economy.
**Why it failed:** the *Music Week* scan archive is not phrase-searchable.
Of 40 issues sampled, only 14 had any text layer, and that OCR is
column-interleaved, so phrases break across lines. Google Books returned
quota errors; archive.org full-text endpoints returned empty.
**To close:** OCR the 159-issue *Music Week* set properly
(`ocrmypdf --deskew --rotate-pages`), then grep for "points",
"production deal", "producer" near "per cent" — the highest-value, cheapest
next step. Also: Kinks/Talmy and Who/Talmy litigation files, which recite
a real independent-producer rate; producer autobiographies (Visconti,
Ken Scott, Alan Parsons).

### 4. Mid-range publishing advance (MEDIUM)
Structure is now well documented — the double 50/50 split leaving the
writer 25% of US income (F47), a 6.25% mechanical rate (F48), and a
£900–£1,500 writer's share on a hit album side (F49). What is missing is
the *typical* advance: the £50 in Schroeder v Macaulay (1974) is a
litigated floor, not a norm (F20).
**To close:** the *Music Week* publishing columns (same OCR job as #3);
the Schroeder case file, whose pleadings cite other real advances as
comparators; Simon Napier-Bell's books, which name figures.

### 5. Agent commission on the promoter side (MEDIUM)
Agency commission is now roughly known for the artist's side — ~10%
established, 15–20% for a first agency (F44) — but whether the agent also
charged the promoter is documented only by a 1977 US handbook (F45).
**To close:** a UK agency's standard terms of business; the Concert
Promoters' Association or Musicians' Union agency agreements at the
Modern Records Centre, Warwick (MU files on agency registration).

### 6. The label/distributor split inside the dealer price (MEDIUM)
The retail end of the chain is now exact (F51, F52): £5.00 retail,
£2.61 dealer ex-VAT, 40% dealer margin, with a 36⅔% standard. What no
1970s UK source does is divide the £2.61 between the label and whoever
distributed it (F53). Five named independent distributors exist in the
record, which proves the tier existed but prices none of it.
**To close:** the Monopolies and Mergers Commission report on the supply
of records; BPI Yearbooks 1975–80 (British Library, Barbican); the
Gramophone Record Retailers Committee papers, which were negotiating
these margins in 1979.

### 7. Price gaps that remain (LOW–MEDIUM)
Round 2 filled 1979 singles (£1.01 Polydor, F54) and corrected the Nov
1975 LP average to £2.85 (F54). Still open: **1976** (the Record Price
Index appears to have lapsed between early 1976 and 1977, so 1976–79
averages must be rebuilt from list prices, not read), **1978 singles**
(bounded by inference between 90p and £1.01), and **1971–72**, which is
*unfillable from this archive*: worldradiohistory holds no 1970–72
Music Week or Record Retailer issues at all.
**To close:** the British Library's trade-paper collection or Billboard's
UK correspondence pages for 1971–72; *Record Retailer* (the title until
it relaunched as *Music Week* on 18 March 1972).

### 8. Used van prices and running costs (MEDIUM)
No 1970s used-Transit price exists in any source found; four strategies
failed, and *Beat Instrumental* carries instrument classifieds only.
Fuel, by contrast, is nailed to an official series (F60). Insurance and
road tax for a young driver: nothing.
**To close:** *Exchange & Mart* 1970–79 (not digitised); *Commercial
Motor* classifieds (British Commercial Vehicle Museum); *Which?* annual
car-cost surveys for insurance and running costs.

### 9. Dole rates for 1970–74 and 1977–79, and the gigging rule (MEDIUM)
The single man's rate is pinned for 1975–76 (£9.80 / £11.10 / £12.90,
F61) and the contribution and availability tests are documented (F62,
F63). Missing: the rate for the first half of the decade and after Nov
1977; supplementary benefit scale rates by year; and — the most valuable
single item — whether gig income was declarable and what the disregard
was. No musician penalty case was found.
**To close:** the annual Social Security Benefits Up-rating Orders on
legislation.gov.uk (each Schedule states the weekly rates verbatim);
Supplementary Benefits Commission annual reports and DHSS *Social
Security Statistics*; Hansard "Unemployment Benefit (Disregards)",
28 January 1972.

### 10. Day-job pay by occupation (MEDIUM)
National averages are solid (F07), but no occupational breakdown was
obtained for the jobs band members actually did — shop assistant,
clerical, factory labourer. Nor is there evidence either way on whether
employers accommodated gigging (shift-swapping, part-time, cash in hand).
**To close:** New Earnings Survey occupational tables 1970–79;
*Department of Employment Gazette* distributions.

### 11. The expensive end of promotion (MEDIUM)
Small-end promotion is now fully priced from printed rate cards: £6.00–
£6.50 a column inch, 5–8p a word, with series discounts (F65). Display
page rates in NME/Melody Maker/Sounds, press-officer retainers, plugger
fees, and poster/handbill unit costs are all still unknown (F66 gives the
major-label ceiling: a reported £200,000 for one album launch).
**To close:** the *Newspaper Press Directory* or *Advertiser's Annual*
1972–79 for display rate tables; IPC/Spotlight media packs at the History
of Advertising Trust; incorporated pluggers' accounts at Companies House.

### 12. Minor open items
- Spiral Scratch cost accounting (~£750 vs ~£500) — F13.
- Sniffin' Glue issue-1 print run (50) vs sales (~300, Christie's) — F25.
- Pistols A&M payout range (£75k "at least" vs ~£125k incl. severance) — F22.
- Undertones' "Teenage Kicks" cost (~£200 commonly cited) — no primary source.
- Whether any proto-indie chart existed in 1979 before Jan 1980 — F31.
- Chart entry copy-count thresholds (the chart is a ~250-shop sample) — F39.
- Rehearsal rates: only two advertised rates found (£4/hr London 1971,
  75p/hr Paignton 1973, F59); the informal end (room above a pub, youth
  club) is unpriced, and advertised rates skew professional.
- PA hire as an alternative to buying: a 1977 advert offers 200W–2000W
  PAs from £10 a day — single advert, worth a follow-up.
- Whether the 36⅔% dealer margin is quoted VAT-inclusive or exclusive:
  the Island case only closes on a VAT-inclusive base, which is an
  inference from arithmetic, not a stated rule (F51, F52).

## Part 4 — Source-quality flags to carry forward

- F26 (press circulations) rests on a single compiled forum study — usable, single-sourced.
- F29 (2,000 vs 20,000 break-even) chains through Wikipedia to Laing's
  *One Chord Wonders*; verify at the book.
- F38's £-threshold basis (retail vs manufacturer revenue) must be stated
  whenever the £ figures are used.
- F44's 15–20% first-agency figure comes from a 1991 retrospective whose
  snippet windows do not fully prove the number attaches to the agency
  sentence — indicative, not settled.
- F51/F52: the 36⅔% and 40% margins are computed on a VAT-inclusive RRP;
  the Island arithmetic only closes that way, so it is an inference from
  arithmetic rather than a stated rule.
- F69 is a c.1980–84 figure and must never be presented as a 1970s price.
- Music Week PDF OCR is imperfect and the archive is not phrase-searchable;
  page-level citations are in `evidence/resolution-prices-and-certification.md`
  and `evidence/round2-retail-chain.md` for re-checking.
- *Beat Instrumental* PDFs are Cloudflare-protected; the working path is
  `https://r.jina.ai/<pdf-url>` for the text layer (recorded in
  `evidence/round2-equipment.md` so it is not rediscovered).
- All famous-act rows in `findings.csv` carry explicit caveats; keep them
  out of beginner balancing.
